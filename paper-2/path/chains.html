Why an unbounded chain still has only rank-one extremes

Why does the Prüfer group alone fail, even though its finite subgroup orders grow without bound?

Proof idea

  1. On a chain, every finite section is a cyclic prime-power case or trivial. Its KD-positive states are mixtures of KD-positive pure states.
  2. Pass from these finite mixtures to the original trace-class state using the finite sections and weak compactness. The passage to the full state space is essential: finite-dimensional rank bounds alone do not control the rank of a limiting state.
  3. An extreme state cannot be a nontrivial barycentre of distinct states. The chain argument yields rank one for every existing extreme point.

What it establishes

Unbounded index provides room but not an obstruction to decomposition. Adding a transverse subgroup direction is the missing condition.
