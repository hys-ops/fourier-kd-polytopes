Why finitely many mixed vertices do not suffice

Polytope classification

The question

A state outside the pure hull only means there must be at least one mixed extreme point. To rule out a polytope, rule out every finite vertex list.

How it works

  1. In a convex decomposition of a singular positive semidefinite matrix, its kernel lies in the kernel of every positively weighted summand.
  2. If only finitely many vertices existed, the singular perturbations would come from a finite collection of vertex supports. Repeated supports along a line force an eigenline of its perturbation direction.
  3. The KD-real Hermitian directions are not covered by those finitely many proper invariant-line constraints. This contradicts finite generation in the nonexceptional groups. The exceptional groups have a finite pure-state hull by the earlier classification.

What carries forward

The conclusion is stronger than “pure states do not suffice.” Lean checks the same classification by a different formal route for the nonpolytope direction.
