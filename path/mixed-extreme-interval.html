A closed interval of mixed extreme states

General strengthening of the finite-vertex obstruction

The question

Nonpolyhedrality gives infinitely many extreme points. Why must their mixed part contain a whole interval rather than just a sequence?

How it works

  1. Only finitely many pure KD-positive states occur for this Fourier pair, so infinitely many extreme states must be mixed.
  2. KD feasibility is semialgebraic, and nonextremality means being the midpoint of two distinct feasible matrices. Projecting those polynomial constraints and taking the complement shows that the extreme locus is semialgebraic. Mixedness adds the inequality Tr(ρ²) < 1.
  3. The written proof obtains an arc from the resulting semialgebraic set. A nontrivial closed subarc has the cardinality of the continuum, which already matches the ambient upper bound. The Lean proof uses the separate polynomial argument explained in the appendix.

What carries forward

The theorem holds for every nonexceptional finite abelian group. The interval theorem itself does not specify a rank or an explicit formula. The manuscript separately cites the exposed-face theorem for spectrahedra; that is not the same as the explicit rank-three construction or its Lean exposure verification.
